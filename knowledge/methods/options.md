# Options framework

## Substantively different = different mechanism

"Discount 10%" and "discount 15%" are one option. Different options change a
different lever: price, audience, product scope, channel, partnership, timing,
or stopping. Test: could two options both be true at once at full strength? If
yes, they may be one plan, not alternatives.

## Mandatory members

- **[STATUS-QUO]** — continue current plan. Describe its trajectory honestly
  (the metric's trend if nothing changes), its cost, and what it keeps open.
- **[STOP]** — discontinue. Freed resources, sunk costs (ignore for the
  decision), customers affected, reputational effect, what is learned.

For an initiative that is **not launched yet**, both would mean "launch
nothing", so separate them: STATUS-QUO = keep today's offer as it is and keep
the idea open; STOP = formally close the initiative for a stated period and
free the team, with a date to revisit.

## Options that contradict the confirmed brief

An option may reverse an answer the user gave at the framing gate (other
segment, other channel) when evidence points there. Include it, mark it
**direction change** in its heading, and let the user decide; never recommend
it silently.

## Steel-manning the counterargument

Write the objection as its best advocate would: the strongest evidence, the
most plausible failure mechanism. "It might be expensive" is weak. "Similar
bundles at two competitors were withdrawn within a year (F) [E-..]" is strong.

## Comparison criteria

Impact on the target metric (range), confidence (based on evidence types),
cost, time to effect, risk, reversibility, fit with constraints in the brief.

## Kill criteria

For each option: the single observation that would make us drop it. These
become research questions or validation stop criteria.

## Decision classes (from gstack autoplan)

- **Mechanical** — one right answer from the brief and evidence → agent decides, states it.
- **Taste** — reasonable people disagree → agent recommends, user decides.
- **Direction change** — changes what the user asked for → never decided by the agent.
