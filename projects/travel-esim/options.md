# Options

Decision (brief): go / no-go on a data-only travel eSIM for own subscribers, and if go, the segment, channel and message for a pilot before summer 2027. Target: 300k paid activations in 12 months, with margin per activation above the roaming data revenue it replaces.

## Situation
- Demand pool is large and growing: 13.4 mln tourist trips in 2025, +15.6% (F) [E-001]; Turkey, UAE, Egypt lead (F) [E-002].
- The space is crowded: MTS sells a travel eSIM (F) [E-003], Beeline sells one (F) [E-004], all big four sell roaming packages for Turkey (F) [E-014, E-015], and independent eSIMs payable with Russian cards cost 1.6x-3.1x less per GB than operator packages (F) [E-021, E-010].
- eSIM is still niche in Russia, only "a few million" users (F) [E-018], but it is growing fast at some operators (F) [E-011, E-012], and travel is a leading use case (F) [E-013].
- The 300k target needs 18.2%-27.3% of our eligible tourist trips if we sell only to our own subscribers (P) [E-038].
- Unknown: P1, whether there is unmet demand (H) [E-024], and P4, whether eSIM margin beats the roaming revenue it replaces (H) [E-025]. Neither can be settled from public data.

## Option A [STATUS-QUO]: Keep the current roaming packages, launch nothing
- **Mechanism**: travellers keep buying our roaming package on their own number; price-sensitive ones leave for independent eSIMs or local SIMs.
- **For**: no new partner, IT or regulatory work (H); the existing packages keep the Russian number with minutes and SMS, which data-only eSIMs lack (F) [E-015].
- **Strongest counterargument**: we are already priced 1.6x-3.1x above independent eSIMs (F) [E-021], and local SIMs are cheaper still (F) [E-008]. Two rivals already sell travel eSIMs (F) [E-003, E-004]. So each season the most price-sensitive travellers' data spend leaks to rivals, and the leak grows as eSIM adoption grows (H) [E-011, E-024].
- **Cost and effort**: zero direct cost.
- **Risks**: gradual loss of travel data revenue; brand looks behind MTS and Beeline (H) [E-003, E-004].
- **What must be true** (H): most of our travellers do not have eSIM-capable phones, or do not bother to switch [E-018].
- **What would change our mind**: internal data showing our roaming data revenue per traveller is falling year on year.

## Option B [STOP]: Formally drop the travel-eSIM initiative for 12 months
- **Mechanism**: close the question, free the product and partner team for other work, revisit in late 2027.
- **For**: P4 is unproven (H) [E-025], and the 300k target looks out of reach from the own base alone (P) [E-038]. The money and team time saved are certain.
- **Strongest counterargument**: the cheapest way to settle P1 and P4 is a small pilot, not a year's wait. Two competitors are already learning in market (F) [E-003, E-004]. Stopping means the 2027 summer peak, the season that matters most for Turkey, Egypt and the UAE, is lost (H) [E-002].
- **Cost and effort**: none; the opportunity cost is one travel season.
- **Risks**: rivals lock in the travel-eSIM habit among our subscribers (H) [E-013].
- **What must be true** (H): margin per eSIM activation is below the roaming revenue it replaces [E-025].
- **What would change our mind**: finance shows a wholesale cost per GB that allows pricing near independents with positive margin.

## Option C: Own-brand data-only travel eSIM, sold in our app to own subscribers, priced near independents
- **Mechanism**: a second, data-only profile bought in our app before departure and paid from the phone balance. It is priced near independents, not near our roaming packages. It acts as a price-discrimination tool: price-sensitive travellers buy it instead of leaving, while the rest stay on roaming.
- **For**: travel is a leading eSIM use case (F) [E-013], and eSIM activations are growing at some operators (F) [E-011, E-012]. Turkey blocks some foreign eSIM sites, so buying before departure in a trusted app is a real advantage (F) [E-009]. Billing to the balance removes a step versus foreign apps (H) [E-020]. The product is data-only, consistent with confirmed premise P2.
- **Strongest counterargument**: MTS and Beeline already do this (F) [E-003, E-004], so it is a me-too product. The device base is thin, with only "a few million" eSIM users (F) [E-018]. Hitting 300k from own subscribers needs an 18.2%-27.3% take-rate of eligible trips, which is very high for a new product (P) [E-038]. Every sale may cannibalise roaming (H) [E-025].
- **Cost and effort**: wholesale partner contract, app purchase flow, support; remote activation must pass subscriber verification (F) [E-019]. Medium effort, about one quarter (H).
- **Risks**: margin below replaced roaming revenue (H) [E-025]; partner blocking in Turkey (F) [E-009]; low take-up because of devices (H) [E-018].
- **What must be true** (H): wholesale cost per GB lets us sell near 52-82 RUB/GB with positive margin [E-021]; at least ~30% of our travellers have eSIM-capable phones; 41% of our base has one (F) [E-034].
- **What would change our mind**: in a pilot, buyers come mostly from current roaming-package buyers instead of from people who would have left (cannibalisation above the margin gain).

## Option D: Cut the price of the existing roaming package, with no eSIM
- **Mechanism**: lower the per-GB price of the current package on the subscriber's own number, closer to independents, to win back leakers without any device requirement.
- **For**: works on every phone, which avoids the eSIM device gap (F) [E-018]. It keeps the number plus minutes and SMS, which data-only eSIMs lack (F) [E-015]. No new partner or verification flow is needed (H) [E-019].
- **Strongest counterargument**: the price cut applies to every traveller, including those who would have paid full price. That is the maximum cannibalisation case, and it directly fails the brief's margin condition (H) [E-025]. It does not count toward the "paid eSIM activations" metric at all.
- **Cost and effort**: low; a tariff change.
- **Risks**: revenue loss across the whole roaming base; a price war with the other three operators (H) [E-015].
- **What must be true** (H): price elasticity of travel data is high enough that volume outweighs the lower price.
- **What would change our mind**: internal elasticity data showing roaming buyers are price-insensitive.

## Option E: Partner-distributed travel eSIM sold to all Russian travellers via travel and bank channels
- **Mechanism**: the same data-only eSIM as C, sold beyond our base through travel booking sites, airlines and bank apps at the moment of booking. This widens the pool from our own subscribers to all 13.4 mln tourist trips.
- **For**: 300k is only 2.24% of tourist trips (F) [E-022], a far more reachable share than the take-rate C needs (P) [E-038]. Non-subscribers bring no roaming revenue to cannibalise (H) [E-025].
- **Strongest counterargument**: this changes the brief's "own subscribers first" choice (Q6), so it is a direction change only you can make. Outside our base we have no billing or app advantage and compete head-on with cheaper independents (F) [E-021], while partners take a commission (H).
- **Cost and effort**: C's build plus partner integrations and commissions; higher (H).
- **Risks**: thin margin after commission; channel conflict.
- **What must be true** (H): a travel or bank partner will give the eSIM placement at the booking step.
- **What would change our mind**: partner commission above the margin per activation.

## Comparison

## Recommendation
Choose **C as a capped summer-2027 pilot**, with E kept as the scale-up path. C is the cheapest way to test P1 and P4 on our own subscribers, and its design limits cannibalisation to travellers who self-select. The evidence also says the 300k target is unlikely to be reached from the own base alone (P) [E-038]. The unknown that matters most is P4: our wholesale cost per GB against the roaming data revenue each buyer used to bring (H) [E-025]. It needs an internal finance number, not more public research.

## User's choice
2026-11-17, PM: **Option C** (own-app data-only eSIM for own subscribers) as the pilot, with **Option E** (partner channels, all travellers) as the scale-up path if the pilot works. Matches the recommendation. PM conditions:
- Finance cannot provide cost per GB before the decision, so P4 stays an open risk that the pilot must measure (H) [E-025, E-030].
- 300k paid activations is not a realistic target for C alone (P) [E-038]; the strategy proposes a separate 12-month pilot target for PM approval (P) [E-037]. The brief's 300k stays the target for C plus E together.
