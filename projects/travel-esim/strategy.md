# Strategy: Travel data eSIM for Russians going abroad

Chosen option: **C** - own-brand data-only travel eSIM, sold in our app to own subscribers, priced near independents, run as a 12-month pilot from summer 2027; **E** (partner channels, all travellers) is the scale-up path (options.md -> User's choice, 2026-11-17).

## Summary for the decision-maker
Approved 2026-11-17 as D-001; reassessed the same day with the finance note (see Change log). Launch C as a 12-month pilot before the summer 2027 season, with a 3% take-rate target instead of 300k; at the measured 41% eSIM-capable share that is about **33k paid activations** (range 26k-40k), down from the 40k approved (P) [E-037]. 300k from own subscribers alone would need 18.2%-27.3% of eligible trips (P) [E-038]. The case: operator travel packages cost 1.6x-3.1x more per GB than independent eSIMs that Russians can pay for (F) [E-021, E-010], so price-sensitive travellers likely leave already, though we have no data on how many (H) [E-024]. The main risk is P4, and the finance note makes it sharper: at 48 RUB/GB and a 790 RUB pack, the eSIM breaks even only if at most 22.9% of buyers would otherwise have bought a roaming package (F) [E-035], while 22% of our trips buy one today (F) [E-033]. The pilot measures the real share against a 20% holdout (H) [E-030, E-039].

## 1. Product strategy
### Target audience and need
- **Segment** (user, brief Q6): our own subscribers with an eSIM-capable phone, going on a 7-14 day leisure trip to Turkey, UAE, Egypt or South-East Asia, who today avoid roaming data. Turkey, UAE and Egypt are the top outbound destinations (F) [E-002].
- **Job-to-be-done**: have mobile data from the moment of landing for maps, taxis and messengers, at a price that does not feel like a penalty, without hunting for a SIM at the airport (H) [E-008, E-009].
- **What they hire today**: a local SIM bought on arrival, which needs a passport or cash (F) [E-008]; an independent eSIM paid by Russian card (F) [E-006, E-010]; a rival operator's travel eSIM (F) [E-003, E-004]; or Wi-Fi only (H). The price bar is set by independents at 52-82 RUB/GB for Turkey (F) [E-021].

### Alternatives the audience has
- Our own roaming package: keeps the number, minutes and SMS, but costs 1.6x-3.1x more per GB than independents (F) [E-015, E-021].
- MTS travel eSIM (200+ countries, 7-30 days) (F) [E-003] and Beeline tourist eSIM (164 countries) (F) [E-004].
- Independent eSIM sellers that accept Russian cards (F) [E-010], with Turkish access to some of their sites restricted since summer 2025 (F) [E-009] (single source).
- Local SIM on arrival (F) [E-008]; doing nothing and living on Wi-Fi (H).

### Chosen direction and deliberate trade-offs
Direction: a second, data-only eSIM profile, bought in our app before departure, paid from the phone balance and priced inside the independent range (H) [E-020, E-021]. It works as price discrimination: travellers who would leave for a cheaper eSIM buy ours; the rest stay on the roaming package (H) [E-024].

We will deliberately **not**:
1. **Sell to non-subscribers in the pilot.** Option E waits until the pilot proves P4, because outside our base we lose the balance-billing edge and pay partner commission (H) [E-020]. No partner work starts before the September 2027 interim read.
2. **Cut the roaming package price** (option D). A blanket cut gives the discount to every traveller, the maximum cannibalisation case (H) [E-025].
3. **Serve phones without eSIM.** They keep the roaming package; the device gap is accepted (F) [E-018].
4. **Bundle voice or SMS.** The product is data-only, as confirmed in P2 (H) [E-005].
5. **Match the cheapest offers.** We price near the top of the independent range, not against local SIMs (F) [E-008, E-021].
6. **Launch all countries at once.** The pilot sells Turkey, UAE, Egypt and two South-East Asian destinations, to keep partner testing and support small (H) [E-002].

### Value, cost and revenue
- **Value to the traveller**: data at 39%-50% less per GB than operator 10 GB packages, bought in a known app and paid from the balance, installed at home before the flight (H) [E-009, E-020, E-021].
- **Revenue mechanism**: a one-off pack price, e.g. Turkey 10 GB at about 790 RUB (79 RUB/GB, near the top of the 52-82 RUB/GB independent range) versus 1,290-1,590 RUB for operator 10 GB packages (P) [E-014, E-015, E-035].
- **Cost drivers**: wholesale data cost, indicatively 48 RUB/GB plus 3% payment processing (F) [E-031]; app purchase flow and subscriber verification for remote activation (F) [E-019]; support for installation; the roaming revenue each buyer used to bring.
- **Unit economics** (F) [E-031, E-035]: at the quoted cost, margin per 790 RUB pack is 286 RUB. At 52 RUB/GB it is only 24 RUB, so the bottom of the independent price range is not viable; the price must stay at or near 79-82 RUB/GB.
- **Break-even condition** (F) [E-032, E-035]: eSIM margin per activation must exceed the roaming revenue it replaces, 1,250 RUB per package trip, so at most 22.9% of buyers may be people who would otherwise have bought a package. Today 22% of our trips buy one (F) [E-033]: if eSIM buyers are average travellers the pilot only breaks even, about +11 RUB per buyer (F) [E-036], and buyers are more likely to skew towards people who buy data today (H) [E-039]. Negotiating wholesale to 40 RUB/GB would lift the limit to 29.3% (P) [E-040].
- **P4 is the main risk the pilot measures** (H) [E-030, E-039]: the cost half is now indicatively known (F) [E-031] and leaves almost no room; the cannibalisation half is measured against the holdout.
- **12-month volume**: at the measured 41% eSIM-capable share, a 3% take-rate gives about 33k paid activations (26k-40k for a 20-30% subscriber share), not the 40k approved in D-001 (P) [E-037]. At about 790 RUB per pack that is roughly 26 mln RUB of eSIM revenue and about 9 mln RUB of margin before cannibalisation (P) [E-035, E-037]; it is a learning pilot, not a revenue line.

### Why 40k, not 300k (pilot target)
- 300k from own subscribers needs 18.2%-27.3% of eligible tourist trips (P) [E-038], far above any rate a new optional add-on can be expected to reach in its first year (H) [E-018].
- No public sales volume for MTS or Beeline travel eSIMs exists to benchmark against (H) [E-027], so the 3% take-rate is our judgement, not a sourced number. That is why the decision rules use the measured **rate**, not the absolute count, which depends on our share of travellers and on whether the 41% base share of eSIM-capable phones holds for travellers (P) [E-037].
- 40k is enough to measure take-rate precisely: at about 1.1 mln exposed trips the standard error of a 3% rate is under 0.02 percentage points; precision for revenue per traveller depends on its variance, which analytics must estimate before launch (P) [E-037].
- The pilot keeps the 300k goal alive as a programme target for C plus E: E would then need about 260k-275k through partners, under the 2.24% of tourist trips that 300k represents (P) [E-022, E-037]. Bank-app channels for travel eSIMs may be growing (H) [E-041] (one seller's claim, headline only).

### Risks to the strategy
- **Demand**: the device base is thin, only "a few million" eSIM users in Russia (F) [E-018]; P1 unmet demand is unproven (H) [E-024].
- **Competitive response**: MTS and Beeline already sell travel eSIMs and could cut prices (F) [E-003, E-004].
- **Regulation and execution**: remote activation needs extra subscriber verification (F) [E-019]; Turkish blocking of foreign eSIM sites may extend to partner provisioning (F) [E-009] (single source); sanctions limits on foreign partners (H).
- **Margin**: P4 unknown (H) [E-025].

## 2. Communication strategy
### Audience and context
The same segment as the product: own subscribers with eSIM-capable phones and a trip coming up. They meet the message in our app and in pre-trip notices, in the days before departure, when they plan connectivity, and must act before the flight because Turkish access to some foreign eSIM sites is restricted (F) [E-009] (single source). Attention is low and the decision is quick, a few hundred roubles.

### Barriers
- **Understanding**: many do not know what an eSIM is or that a second data profile can sit next to their SIM; eSIM is still niche in Russia (F) [E-018].
- **Relevance**: "is my phone compatible?"; budget phones often lack eSIM (F) [E-018].
- **Trust and effort**: installation feels technical, and remote activation needs extra verification (F) [E-019].
- **Price**: independents are cheaper still at the bottom of their range, and local SIMs are cheaper again (F) [E-008, E-021].

### Key message
"Travel data for less than a roaming package: buy it in our app, pay from your balance, and install it at home before you fly." (H) [E-020, E-021]. The exact saving (39%-50% per GB for a 10 GB pack) goes on the product page, not in the headline, until the pilot price is fixed (P) [E-035]. The message addresses understanding and effort first, then price; no customer wording is available to borrow (no interviews), so it must be tested (H).

### Proof points
- Per GB, our pack is priced inside the independent range, 39%-50% below operator 10 GB packages (P) [E-021, E-035] - true once the pilot price is set.
- Paid from the phone balance in our app, no foreign card or foreign site needed (H) [E-020].
- Install at home: works on landing, and avoids access problems with foreign eSIM sites in Turkey (F) [E-009] (single source).
- Your main SIM stays in the phone; only the data line is new (H) [E-005].

### Channels and their roles
- **Awareness - pre-trip notice to subscribers with past roaming in pilot countries and an eSIM-capable phone**: our own data tells us who travels and who can use it; the notice must come before departure (H) [E-009].
- **Explanation - product page in the app with a phone-compatibility check**: answers the understanding and relevance barriers in the place the purchase happens (H) [E-018].
- **Conversion - in-app purchase paid from the balance**: removes the payment step, our main edge (H) [E-020].
- **Onboarding - step-by-step install guide and support chat**: tackles effort and the verification step (F) [E-019].
- **Retention - reminder before the next trip to buyers**: repeat purchase is cheaper than new acquisition (H).
- Not in the pilot: travel and bank partners (option E), kept for scale-up (H) [E-041].

## 3. Validation plan

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | If eSIM-capable subscribers travelling to the five pilot destinations are offered a data-only eSIM in our app at 79-82 RUB/GB, at least 3% of their trips will include a paid eSIM, and total revenue minus eSIM wholesale cost per exposed traveller will be no lower than roaming revenue per traveller in the holdout, because the eSIM mostly catches travellers who would otherwise leave for independents (H) [E-024, E-035, E-037]. |
| Primary metric | Take-rate: paid eSIM activations / trips by eligible exposed subscribers to pilot destinations (trip = roaming registration abroad of the main SIM, or eSIM activation abroad, whichever comes first, de-duplicated, because buyers may switch the main SIM off; eligible = eSIM-capable device), monthly, from billing and network data. Target 3% over 12 months (D-001); at 41% eSIM-capable this is about 33k activations, not the 40k in D-001, pending PM decision on a superseding entry (P) [E-037]. |
| Baseline | Random 20% holdout of eligible subscribers who are not shown the offer: comparable by design. It gives the roaming revenue per traveller without the eSIM, which is the counterfactual for P4. Starting baseline: 0 activations (brief). |
| Sample / MDE | About 1.1 mln exposed and 275k holdout eligible trips in the mid case (P) [E-037]. Smallest effect worth acting on: a 1 percentage-point change in take-rate, and a 5% difference in revenue per traveller between arms. The take-rate effect is easily detectable even in the low case (0.88 mln exposed); the revenue difference needs a variance estimate from last year's roaming data before launch. |
| Observation period | 12 months from launch (target May 2027), with a pre-set interim read at end of September 2027 after the summer peak, and a pre-launch check when the partner contract is priced. |
| Guardrail metrics | (1) Cannibalisation: share of buyers who bought a roaming package in the previous 12 months, and roaming revenue per traveller exposed vs holdout; must stay below the break-even share k_max, 22.9% at the quoted cost and 790 RUB (F) [E-035]; read monthly from the first month, because the expected level (22%) is already at the limit (F) [E-033]. (2) Activation failure rate (verification, blocking) [E-009, E-019]. (3) Support contacts per activation. (4) Refunds and complaints. (5) Churn of exposed vs holdout travellers. |
| Decision rule | **Before launch**: if the contract's wholesale cost gives k_max below 15% at the planned price, do not launch at that price; reprice or stop. **September 2027 interim**: stop if take-rate < 1.5%, or if net revenue per exposed traveller is more than 5% below the holdout and the difference is statistically significant. **Month 12**: scale to E if take-rate >= 3%, net revenue per exposed traveller >= holdout, and activation success >= 90%; extend 6 months if take-rate is 1.5%-3% with P4 passing; otherwise stop. |
| Data check | Billing and network analytics produce take-rate and revenue by arm monthly; finance validates wholesale cost per GB from partner invoices each month and signs off P4 at the interim and final reads. |

Message testing: in the pilot app page, A/B test the price-led line against the "install at home before you fly" line on product-page-to-purchase conversion; a five-user comprehension check of the install guide before launch (H).

## Known limitations
- P4 is unproven: finance gave an indicative wholesale quote and roaming revenue per package trip (F) [E-031, E-032], which leave a break-even cannibalisation share of 22.9% (F) [E-035]; the actual share among buyers is unknown until the pilot (H) [E-039]. The finance note is marked as test data.
- The 3% take-rate and 40k target rest on our judgement and on hypothesised subscriber and eSIM-capable shares; there is no public benchmark (H) [E-027].
- Most price data comes from seller blogs at reliability C, not checked on operator sites; the Turkish blocking claim is single-source (F) [E-009, E-021].
- No real customer research: the message is untested and comes from evidence on barriers, not customer words (H).
- Agent spend could not be measured in this session (cost.json Stop hook did not run).
- The bank-channel growth signal for option E is one seller's headline, year not visible, reliability D (H) [E-041]; it does not support E on its own.
- "Main SIM stays; only data is added" rests on a single user review of a rival product (H) [E-005]; to be confirmed on the partner's profile during build.
- Targeting travellers by their past roaming data needs a legal check of subscriber consent for marketing use (H).
- The P4 test compares eSIM margin with replaced roaming **revenue**, as the brief defines it; this is conservative, because roaming revenue also carries cost.
